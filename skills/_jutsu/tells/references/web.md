# Tells - web

The concrete web material for the `tells` module. Every entry has the three fields the module
defines, and only those. Read the module's rule first: a pattern the validated thesis names is not
a tell, and a mood word names nothing.

Sources: observations of landing pages generated by current models with no design guidance, and
the tells catalogue of taste-skill (Leonxlnx, MIT), rewritten here as evidence and questions.

## Invented information

### Weather or locale strip
- **Marker:** a city code, a clock and a temperature strung together in a header or footer, such
  as `LIS 14:23 · 18°C`, or a timezone badge like `GMT+1`.
- **Why it is a tell:** models add it to make a studio feel inhabited. The city, the hour and the
  weather are chosen for atmosphere, not read from anything.
- **The question:** does the project work across time zones in a way the visitor needs to see,
  and does the thesis say so?

### Fake build status
- **Marker:** release strings and sync times on a page that is not a changelog: `v0.6.2-rc.1`,
  `last sync 4s ago`, `Build 0048`, `BETA`.
- **Why it is a tell:** it borrows the texture of developer tools to look shipped and alive. The
  numbers are made up and stay frozen forever.
- **The question:** which real release does this number track, and where does the page read it?

### Fake heritage
- **Marker:** a founding year worn as a badge: `ESTD. 2018`, `Since 2016`, `Founded MMXIX`.
- **Why it is a tell:** an established date is the quickest way to borrow trust, so models
  invent one when the brief gives none.
- **The question:** is this the project's actual founding year, and does the visitor need it?

### Fake stock counter
- **Marker:** a live-looking scarcity count: `Reservation 412 of 800`, `Only 3 left`,
  `27 people viewing`.
- **Why it is a tell:** urgency is a conversion reflex. With no inventory behind it, the counter
  is a claim the page cannot keep.
- **The question:** which system provides this number, and does it change when stock does?

### Fake photo credit
- **Marker:** gallery captions that credit nobody real: `Plate 03 · House archive`,
  `Field study no. 12`, `Frame XII · 35mm` under stock or placeholder images.
- **Why it is a tell:** captions make a placeholder image look curated. The photographer, the
  archive and the film stock do not exist.
- **The question:** who took this photograph, and has the project the right to credit them?

### Placeholder names and brands
- **Marker:** `John Doe`, `Jane Smith`, `Acme`, `Nexus`, `lorem ipsum`, testimonials signed by
  people who were never asked.
- **Why it is a tell:** these are the first names in the model's memory for "a person" and "a
  company". Invented but plausible names are the same tell, only harder to spot.
- **The question:** is this a real client, a real quote, a real name, with permission to show it?

### Round or falsely precise figures
- **Marker:** `99.99% uptime`, `10,000+ teams`, `10x faster`, `100% satisfaction`, or the reverse
  disguise, `47.3%` with no source anywhere on the page.
- **Why it is a tell:** round numbers are what a model writes when it needs a number; odd decimals
  are what it writes when told to avoid round ones. Neither was measured.
- **The question:** where was this figure measured, and could the page cite that source?

### Fake product drawn in divs
- **Marker:** a hero "screenshot" built from styled boxes: a dashboard, a terminal, a task list
  or a code editor that renders no real product.
- **Why it is a tell:** models cannot see the product, so they draw what a product usually looks
  like. The result shows the category, not the thing being sold.
- **The question:** does a real screenshot, recording or component of the product exist, and if
  not, does the page need a picture of it at all?

## Decorative filler

### Numbered eyebrow
- **Marker:** a zero-padded index above each section title: `00 / INDEX`, `001 · Capabilities`,
  `04. Process`.
- **Why it is a tell:** numbering reads as editorial rigour, so models number everything. Nobody
  navigates a landing page by section number.
- **The question:** does the visitor use these numbers to find or cite a section?

### Tile pagination
- **Marker:** counters on images or bento tiles, like `01 / 4`, when all four are visible at once.
- **Why it is a tell:** it copies the chrome of a slideshow onto content that does not page.
- **The question:** can the visitor move between these items, and does the counter change when
  they do?

### Generic step labels
- **Marker:** `Stage 1 / Stage 2 / Stage 3`, `Step 01`, `Phase II`, `Pass One` used as the title of
  each step.
- **Why it is a tell:** the model knows a process needs steps before it knows what the steps are,
  so the label is written first and the content fitted in after.
- **The question:** what does the visitor actually do at each step, and is the number doing any
  work the order does not already do?

### Word strip at the bottom of the hero
- **Marker:** three or four capitalised words in a row under the hero: `TYPE / FORM / MOTION`,
  `DESIGN · BUILD · SHIP`.
- **Why it is a tell:** a portfolio cliché models reproduce to fill the space between the call
  to action and the fold. It lists disciplines nobody asked about.
- **The question:** do these words link anywhere or tell the visitor something the headline does
  not?

### Scroll cue
- **Marker:** `Scroll`, `Scroll to explore`, a bouncing arrow or a mouse-wheel animation at the
  bottom of the first screen.
- **Why it is a tell:** it fills the lower edge of the hero with a gesture everyone already knows.
- **The question:** is there evidence that visitors of this page stop at the first screen?

### Decorative status dots
- **Marker:** a small coloured dot before nav items, list rows, badges or `Available for work`,
  often pulsing.
- **Why it is a tell:** a status light suggests a live system. Models add it for motion and
  colour, with no state behind it.
- **The question:** which real state does this dot report, and what turns it off?

### Pills laid on images
- **Marker:** tags or labels positioned over photographs: `Brand · 02`, `New`, `Case study`.
- **Why it is a tell:** it layers the look of a tagged archive onto images that carry no tags.
- **The question:** does this label hold information the visitor cannot get from the image or its
  caption?

### Middle-dot runs
- **Marker:** three or more items joined by middle dots on one line: `Brand · Motion · Spatial ·
  Lisbon`.
- **Why it is a tell:** the middle dot is the model's default separator for anything that looks
  like metadata, so it spreads to lines that are not metadata.
- **The question:** are these items a real list the visitor scans, and does the thesis ask for
  them on one line?

### Decorative grids and crosshairs
- **Marker:** hairline grids, corner crosshairs or registration marks drawn behind content that
  does not align to them.
- **Why it is a tell:** printer's marks signal craft. Drawn over a layout that ignores them, they
  are wallpaper.
- **The question:** does any content sit on this grid, and does the thesis name the print
  reference?

### Vertical text
- **Marker:** a label rotated ninety degrees along an edge: `INDEX OF WORK`, `SCROLL`, a year.
- **Why it is a tell:** rotated type is the fastest way to look experimental, and models reach for
  it whenever a brief says creative.
- **The question:** what does the rotation let the visitor read that horizontal text would not?

### Micro-sentences under eyebrows
- **Marker:** a short explanatory sentence between the eyebrow and the heading, or right under the
  heading, restating the section: "Everything we ship, nothing we promise."
- **Why it is a tell:** models pad each section with a line of framing because every section in
  their training looks framed.
- **The question:** if this sentence were removed, would the visitor lose a fact?

## Reflex convergence

### Three equal cards
- **Marker:** a three-column row of cards with the same size, the same structure and the same class
  list, usually an icon, a title and two lines.
- **Why it is a tell:** it is the model's first layout for "features". Three is the number that
  looks complete, whatever the content.
- **The question:** does the content really come in three items of equal weight?

### Repeated zigzag
- **Marker:** image left, text right, then the reverse, more than twice in a row.
- **Why it is a tell:** alternation is the cheapest way to make repetition look designed.
- **The question:** does each section's content call for an image beside it, or is the image there
  to keep the rhythm?

### One layout family everywhere
- **Marker:** every section built from the same arrangement: centred heading, then a grid; or
  heading left, content right; section after section.
- **Why it is a tell:** once a model finds a structure that works it reuses it, since every section
  is generated with the same prompt pressure.
- **The question:** which layout family does the thesis name, and where does a section's content
  differ enough to need another?

### Bento with an empty cell
- **Marker:** a bento grid with a cell that holds only a gradient, a pattern or nothing at all.
- **Why it is a tell:** the grid shape was chosen before the content was counted, so a cell is left
  over.
- **The question:** how many items does the project have for this grid, and does the shape match
  that count?

### Headline with a paragraph floating in the corner
- **Marker:** a large heading on one side and a small paragraph pinned to the opposite top corner,
  aligned to nothing.
- **Why it is a tell:** it imitates a studio portfolio header. The paragraph drifts because nothing
  in the layout asked for it.
- **The question:** what does the paragraph align to, and does it need to sit apart from the
  heading it explains?

### Hairlines on every row
- **Marker:** `border-t` and `border-b` on each row of a list or specification table, so that every
  line is boxed.
- **Why it is a tell:** rules on every row are the default way to make a list look precise.
- **The question:** does the visitor need to track across columns here, and does the thesis name
  this ruling?

### Score bars on a full track
- **Marker:** comparison bars drawn as a partial fill over a full grey track, for skills, features
  or competitors.
- **Why it is a tell:** it lifts a dashboard component onto a marketing page to make claims look
  measured.
- **The question:** what was measured to produce these lengths, and against what maximum?

### Display face by reflex
- **Marker:** a display face the thesis never names: an editorial serif (Fraunces or Instrument
  Serif most often), frequently with one italic accent word, or a heavy grotesque sans set tight
  and large.
- **Why it is a tell:** models map mood words to a face. Creative, premium or editorial calls up
  the free display serifs; clean, modern or SaaS calls up the heavy grotesque. Either way the face
  was summoned by adjectives, not decided, and removing one reflex tends to install the other.
- **The question:** which sentence of the thesis names this typeface, and says why this one for
  this product?

### Warm paper ground
- **Marker:** a faintly warm off-white background with near-black ink and a single red or rust
  accent, on a brief that never mentions paper, print or ink.
- **Why it is a tell:** it is the current default for "tasteful". It looks considered on every
  brief, so it says nothing about this one.
- **The question:** what in the product or the brief makes paper the right ground, and does the
  thesis say so?

### Gradient text
- **Marker:** headings filled with a gradient: `bg-clip-text text-transparent`, or
  `background-clip: text` in CSS.
- **Why it is a tell:** a gradient fill is the quickest way to make a heading look branded, so it
  lands on headings whatever the palette.
- **The question:** is this gradient part of the palette the thesis validated, and does it survive
  the contrast check on every stop?

### U+2014 (em dash)
- **Marker:** the character U+2014 (em dash) in visible copy, typed or written as `&mdash;`,
  `&#8212;` or `&#x2014;`.
- **Why it is a tell:** models join clauses with it far more often than the people whose writing
  they learned from. At that density it is a signature, not punctuation.
- **The question:** does the project's own copy, or the thesis, use this character? If not, what
  put it on this page?

## Hollow copy

### Filler verbs
- **Marker:** `Elevate`, `Seamless`, `Unleash`, `Next-Gen`, `Supercharge`, `Empower`,
  `Revolutionize` in headings and calls to action.
- **Why it is a tell:** these verbs fit every product, which is why the model reaches for them
  when it knows nothing about this one.
- **The question:** what does the product do, in the words its users would use?

### One intent, several labels
- **Marker:** buttons and links that ask for the same thing under different words:
  `Get started`, `Start free trial` and `Sign up` on one page.
- **Why it is a tell:** each section is written on its own, and each gets a fresh phrasing of the
  same call to action.
- **The question:** what is the one action this page asks for, and what does the product call it?

### Poetic section labels
- **Marker:** `From the field`, `Field notes`, `On the bench`, `Loose plates` over testimonials,
  articles or updates.
- **Why it is a tell:** the model dresses functional sections in craft vocabulary to sound
  handmade.
- **The question:** would a visitor looking for this content search for these words?

### Mannered social proof
- **Marker:** `Quietly trusted by`, `In good company`, `Loved by the best` above a logo wall.
- **Why it is a tell:** false modesty is the model's idea of confidence. The adverb carries the
  claim so that the logos do not have to.
- **The question:** are these logos real clients who agreed to appear, and what do they use the
  product for?

### Register drift
- **Marker:** a page whose tone changes between sections: technical in one, lyrical in the next,
  salesy in the footer.
- **Why it is a tell:** sections are generated one after another, and each drifts toward whatever
  voice its subject most often has in the training data.
- **The question:** which voice does the thesis set, and does every section read in it?

### Unread strings
- **Marker:** any visible string (heading, label, alt text, button, footer line) that nobody read
  again after it was written.
- **Why it is a tell:** copy produced in one pass keeps the defaults of every family above. They
  surface on a second read and hide on the first.
- **The question:** has every visible string on this page been read once more, against the thesis,
  before calling it finished?
